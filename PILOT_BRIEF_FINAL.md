1. WHAT YOUR PILOT INCLUDES (FINAL, LOCKED)

Your pilot includes only what is required to safely move a student from request → supervised training → documented completion.

✅ INCLUDED IN THE PILOT

A. Student → Hospital intake
	•	Student submits:
	•	basic details
	•	CV / student letter
	•	dates
	•	OR enters allocation (EHS / DHA / DoH case)

No essays. No scoring. No advice.

⸻

B. Regulatory handling (quiet, background)
	•	If no regulator → nothing special
	•	If EHS → reference recorded
	•	If DHA / DoH → workflow pauses until reference entered and hospital confirms

No integrations. No verification logic.

⸻

C. Hospital decision
	•	Hospital can:
	•	Accept (conditional)
	•	Defer
	•	Decline

That’s it.

⸻

D. Department & supervisor assignment
	•	Hospital assigns:
	•	department
	•	supervisor

Manual is fine.

⸻

E. Exposure definition (CRITICAL)
	•	Exposure level defined as:
	•	Observation only
	•	Limited participation

Clear. Explicit. Immutable once acknowledged.

⸻

F. Student exposure acknowledgement (CRITICAL)
	•	Student must explicitly acknowledge exposure limits
	•	Timestamped
	•	Training cannot start without this

⸻

G. Supervisor confirmation (CRITICAL)
	•	Supervisor explicitly confirms:
	•	responsibility
	•	exposure boundaries
	•	Timestamped
	•	Training cannot start without this

⸻

H. Completion record
	•	Supervisor confirms completion
	•	Dates + exposure recorded

No evaluation. No certificates.

⸻

I. Simple trainee list (hospital view)
	•	Student
	•	Department
	•	Supervisor
	•	Dates
	•	Exposure
	•	Regulatory status

Read-only.

⸻

❌ NOT INCLUDED (ON PURPOSE)
	•	No student advice (“best for you if…”)
	•	No AI
	•	No rankings
	•	No reviews
	•	No messaging
	•	No payments
	•	No dashboards
	•	No future promises in the app

⸻

🔑 ONE-LINE DEFINITION OF THE PILOT (INTERNAL)

The pilot documents exposure expectations and supervision clearly, without changing hospital workflows.

If something doesn’t serve that → it’s out.

2. WHAT YOU SAY WHEN PITCHING HOSPITALS

Hospital Pitch (First Meeting)

Opening (Problem)

“We noticed that after students are approved or allocated, hospitals often don’t have written proof of what the student was allowed to do or which consultant agreed to supervise them.
That creates confusion later — especially if expectations don’t match.”

Pause. Let them agree. They usually will.

⸻

What Electivio is (Reframed correctly)

“Electivio is not about efficiency or replacing any regulator.
It’s a small coordination layer that records three things clearly:

	1. what exposure was agreed
	2. who approved supervision
	3. that regulatory requirements were respected”

⸻

What the pilot is

“We’re proposing a small, no-cost pilot focused only on documenting exposure expectations and supervision in one or two departments.
We’re not changing how you work — we’re just making it explicit and recorded.”

⸻

What it is NOT (this matters)

“This is not a rollout, not an IT system, and not a commitment.
If it’s not useful, you stop.”

This sentence lowers resistance massively.

⸻

If they ask about payment

“We don’t charge for pilots.
Pricing is only discussed if this proves genuinely useful for your team.”

Stop talking.

⸻

Close

“If this gives you clearer records and fewer misunderstandings, we continue.
If not, we part ways cleanly.”

That’s it.

⸻

3. WHAT YOU SAY WHEN PITCHING STUDENTS

Student Pitch (Pilot Phase)

Opening

“Electivio doesn’t promise hands-on training or placements.
It helps make sure expectations are clear before you start — so you’re not surprised later.”

⸻

What Electivio does for them

“Before training begins, Electivio shows you:

	• what you’re actually allowed to do
	• who your supervising consultant is
	• whether regulatory clearance is required

You acknowledge this upfront, so there are no misunderstandings.”

⸻

What it does NOT do (important)

“Electivio does not rank hospitals, advise you on what’s best for your career, or guarantee outcomes — especially during the pilot.”

This protects you.

⸻

Why students should still care

“This protects you from:

	• unclear roles
	• false expectations
	• wasted time in placements that aren’t what you thought”

⸻

Close

“The pilot is about clarity, not promises.”

⸻

FINAL TRUTH (READ THIS SLOWLY)
	• The pilot is small on purpose
	• It must feel safe, boring, and obvious
	• Hospitals accept it because it reduces risk
	• Students accept it because it reduces uncertainty

You are not selling a platform yet.
You are earning permission.
